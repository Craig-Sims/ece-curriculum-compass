Curriculum as a Compass for Learning in Early Childhood Education

Kurikulum sebagai Kompas Pembelajaran di Pendidikan Anak Usia Dini

A programme for early childhood educators on translating the national curriculum into everyday classroom practice.

Program bagi pendidik PAUD untuk menerjemahkan kurikulum nasional ke dalam praktik pembelajaran sehari-hari.

What this programme is forTujuan program ini

Early childhood educators already know their children and their communities. This programme helps you translate the curriculum into practice: from a curriculum goal to a plan, an experience, evidence of learning, a next step and an improvement. You bring your own curriculum. We help with the moves in between.

By the end of the programme you will be able to…Pada akhir program Anda akan dapat…

  1. Explain the curriculum kurikulum as a compass kompas that gives direction for planning, teaching and assessment, and not a script or an administrative record.
  2. Plan learning experiences that weave together children’s development in a holistic and integrated holistik dan terpadu way, starting from a curriculum goal.
  3. Adapt learning so that it responds to each child (child-centred berpusat pada anak), changing how children take part while keeping the learning goal.
  4. Observe, document and interpret children’s learning (assessment asesmen) and use it to decide the next teaching step.
  5. Evaluate how the curriculum is used in their own setting and produce a realistic action plan rencana tindak lanjut.
  6. Bring these together by showing one curriculum goal travelling from intention to experience, evidence and improvement.

The modulesModul

Each module has about 90 minutes of online work you do on your own, then a 3-hour live session with a break halfway.

1

Curriculum as a Living Guide for LearningKurikulum sebagai Panduan Pembelajaran yang Hidup

Professor Karen Murcia

What you will be able to do
  1. Explain how a curriculum (kurikulum) works as a compass (kompas): it gives direction without prescribing every step.
  2. Describe the four layers of curriculum (intended, planned, enacted, experienced) and spot where learning can be lost between them.
  3. Judge whether a classroom episode shows a living curriculum, using evidence of what children do, think and learn.
  4. Name one real challenge in how the curriculum is used in their own setting.

2

Planning for Holistic Foundational DevelopmentPerencanaan untuk Perkembangan Fondasi yang Holistik

Dr Emma Cross

What you will be able to do
  1. Explain what holistic development means in early childhood education and why areas of development are interconnected.
  2. Recognise opportunities for multiple aspects of children’s development within everyday play, routines, inquiry and learning experiences.
  3. Use curriculum goals or intentions to develop a clear focus for learning and a lens for observation without fragmenting the child’s experience into isolated domains.
  4. Design or refine an integrated learning experience that intentionally supports multiple aspects of children’s development.
  5. Reflect on how holistic planning can be adapted to their own children, setting, community and curriculum context.
Open the online part
3

Child-centred Learning: Responding to Every ChildPembelajaran yang Berpusat pada Anak: Menanggapi Setiap Anak

Dr Craig Sims

What you will be able to do
  1. Describe what child-centred and developmentally appropriate practice looks like in a classroom, starting from children’s interests, strengths and needs.
  2. Notice a child’s capability and what may get in the way of taking part, before looking at labels.
  3. Adjust a planned experience so every child can take part and show understanding, changing the pathway but keeping the learning goal.
  4. Weigh where technology, including unplugged activities, can personalise learning and where it gets in the way.
  5. Decide what to watch for, to tell whether a support is helping or hiding a child’s learning.
Open the online part
4

Assessment for Learning in Early Childhood EducationAsesmen untuk Pembelajaran di PAUD

Associate Professor Sonja Kuzich

What you will be able to do
  1. Explain the principles of authentic assessment in early childhood education: formative assessment first; observation as a primary data source; assessment that is fair, objective and educational; assessment as part of the teaching and learning cycle.
  2. Observe and document children’s learning using techniques suited to early childhood, separating description from interpretation.
  3. Interpret documented evidence holistically against the three learning outcome elements (CP), identifying strengths and next teaching steps.
  4. Use assessment information to plan responsive learning experiences and communicate children’s progress to families in clear, strengths-based language.
Open the online part
5

Strengthening Curriculum ImplementationMemperkuat Pelaksanaan Kurikulum

Dr Tamara Bromley

What you will be able to do
  1. Reflect upon and evaluate the effectiveness of curriculum implementation in your setting, drawing on a variety of evidence
  2. Identify barriers and opportunities to strengthen curriculum implementation in your setting
  3. Explain the notion of quality as it applies in your setting, and how strengthening curriculum implementation is part of increasing quality.
  4. Describe the important place of reflective practice when aiming to improve quality and strengthen curriculum implementation
  5. Produce a realistic action plan to increase quality and strengthen curriculum implementation
